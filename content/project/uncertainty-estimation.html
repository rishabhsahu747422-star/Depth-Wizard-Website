2. Methods & Algorithms

Innovation 2: Test-Time Augmentation & Uncertainty Mapping

Quantifying epistemic confidence to communicate height estimation reliability for disaster decision-making.

1. Operational Necessity of Confidence Metrics

In real-world applications such as flood levee assessment or building height verification, presenting an elevation model as universally accurate can lead to disastrous decision-making. High-frequency occlusions, cast shadows, and specular water reflections naturally induce model uncertainty.

2. Why Monte Carlo Dropout Was Rejected

The initial design for this module followed the standard Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout formulation: keep dropout layers active at inference and treat the variance across repeated stochastic passes as an epistemic uncertainty signal. Testing this against the fine-tuned backbone on a real GAMUS tile showed it does not work for this model. The backbone's attention_probs_dropout_prob, hidden_dropout_prob, and drop_path_rate are all 0 by default, so repeated stochastic passes are numerically near-identical (variance below 1e-8) — the resulting "confidence map" collapses to a flat, meaningless constant everywhere. Dynamically forcing dropout on at inference was considered but rejected: it would require perturbing a component the model was never fine-tuned with dropout active in, making the resulting variance unrepresentative of genuine prediction ambiguity rather than an artifact of the injected noise.

3. Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) Sampling

The module was reworked around test-time augmentation instead: the same input tile is passed through the backbone under N=8 fixed, deterministic perturbations — identity, horizontal flip, vertical flip, both flips combined, and four brightness/gamma shifts — rather than random internal noise. Each output is un-flipped back to the original orientation before aggregation, so all 8 predictions are pixel-aligned:

σ²(x, y) = (1 / N) ∑_{i=1}^N [ h_i(x, y) - μ(x, y) ]²

Because the perturbations are external (input-space) rather than internal (weight-space), the variance reflects genuine sensitivity of the depth prediction to viewpoint- and illumination-like changes, not an artifact of a dropout mechanism the model was never trained to tolerate. The resulting variance map is exported as a normalized confidence texture. On a real held-out GAMUS test tile, this produced a variance range of 0.0004–81.3 (mean 6.2) — a genuinely structured signal, not a flat fallback — with confidence visibly lowest at building and tree-canopy edges, exactly where height discontinuities make the prediction hardest.

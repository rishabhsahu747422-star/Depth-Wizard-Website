Key Findings

What this project demonstrates

01

Foundation models transfer to nadir geometry

A general-purpose monocular depth model adapts to top-down remote-sensing imagery through targeted fine-tuning, without training from scratch — a 40% overall MAE reduction against zero-shot on the v3 checkpoint.
02

Per-class calibration measurably beats a global fit

Calibrating scale independently per land-cover class reduced error by roughly 91% against a single global affine fit in a controlled synthetic test, confirming the semantic-context finding of Srivastava et al. (2017) in this specific pipeline.
03

Test-time augmentation is a real substitute for Monte Carlo Dropout

Where a backbone’s dropout configuration makes MC-Dropout numerically meaningless, input-space test-time augmentation produces a genuinely structured confidence signal instead — a finding likely relevant beyond this project.
04

GSD normalisation is required, not optional

Without ground-sample-distance normalisation, the model silently produces plausible-looking but structurally meaningless output on imagery at the wrong effective resolution — confirmed on real Sentinel-2 imagery.
Impact & Implications

Who this is for

The intended user is a scientist, disaster-response planner, or geospatial analyst who has a single optical image of a site and needs an approximate 3D terrain model without waiting on a LiDAR survey or stereo satellite tasking. The system is explicitly not intended to replace survey-grade elevation data where that is available and precision matters — it fills the gap when it is not.

Disaster response

A first approximate terrain model for a site immediately after a single image becomes available, before a survey can be organised.

Environmental & infrastructure planning

Quick terrain context for a site under study, usable alongside — not instead of — formal survey data where precision is required.
Limitations

Stated plainly

No LiDAR ground truth exists for the real-world generalisation tests (Delhi, Kolkata, Gangotri) — those results are plausibility checks, not validated accuracy.
Single-tile calibration shows near-zero affine slopes for most classes on real data — robust use of pixel-level calibration signal is demonstrated so far only in controlled synthetic testing.
The depth backbone’s raw output resolution limits how much edge-aware post-filtering can sharpen a mesh; it can realign existing signal but cannot recover detail the backbone never predicted.
No public GPU inference deployment: a local, standalone research tool by design, given no compute budget for hosted inference — a stated constraint, not an overlooked gap.
